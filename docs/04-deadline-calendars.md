# Chapter 4 — Deadline Calendars

> One sentence to remember: **Deadlines are the only fight they can win without talking to you.**

---

## Why this chapter exists

More consumer claims die of a quiet missed deadline than of any argument. Warranty windows close, appeal clocks run out, and "reply by" dates get skipped — and none of it happens because someone was wrong. It happens because *the calendar was vague.*

This chapter fixes the vagueness. It's a system, not a list of laws (Chapter 1 says why we don't list laws: they change and they vary — your job here is to track *your specific* deadlines, not memorize categories).

## The four clocks to copy into every dispute folder

When a dispute opens, find and write down these four numbers *before* doing anything else:

| Clock | Where it usually comes from | Where you write it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Return/exchange window** | Store policy page, order confirmation email | In `00-log.md`, as "Return window closes: `<date>`" |
| **Warranty window** | Warranty card/policy text, seller listing | Same |
| **Dispute/appeal window** | The letter, denial letter, card issuer on the phone | Same — this is the *big* one for insurance |
| **Regulator or small-claims limit window** | The regulator's/office's own published info | Same |

Rule of thumb: **any window that's measured from a past event shrinks every single day.** "60 days from denial" on a denial that's already 50 days old is a different situation than on a fresh one — check the *actual* date on the letter, not the category.

## Your calendar, three ways (pick one, actually use one)

### Option A — companion file in the dispute folder
Add five lines to `00-log.md` the day the dispute opens:

```markdown
## Deadlines
- Return window closes: 2026-04-13
- Warranty (14 mo from purchase): 2027-05-14
- Issuer dispute window: ask issuer — call today (or start of month)
- Regulator complaint window: check site — write date here
- My reply-by for letter #1: 2026-03-18
```

### Option B — real calendar entries
Drop each deadline into your phone/calendar with a name that starts with the dispute, e.g. `DISPUTE: Main St Coffee — return window closes 2026-04-13`. Put two reminders on each: one 7 days before, one 2 days before. Calendar apps do this for free, and "future you" doesn't remember why an alert is pinging.

### Option C — a wall calendar, paper and twenty minutes
For low-button-click people (this manual loves you too): print this chapter's blank template from the [site](../site/index.html#templates) and hang it by the coffee maker. The dispute that lives on the wall is the dispute that gets handled.

## The two-touch rule (the calendar habit that pays)

For every deadline you write down, put **two reminders** in your system:

1. **T-minus 7 days** — "am I still planning to act on this?"
2. **T-minus 2 days** — "do it now or consciously decide to drop it"

A reminder you snooze past is nearly as bad as no reminder — the second touch exists because we all snooze.

## The follow-up clock (for the ladder in Chapter 2)

Every letter you send starts *its own* clock:

- **Reply due date** — 7–14 calendar days from sending; write it in the letter itself and in `00-log.md`.
- **Follow-up date** — reply date + 3 days. That's when Letter #2 goes out the door automatically.
- For a running dispute, aim for one contact per week while climbing. Slower reads as "they gave up"; faster reads as noise (and can genuinely annoy a clerk who is actually working on your file).

## Once a deadline is *missed*

1. Don't panic and don't hide. Determine whether it was a "hard" deadline (a legal/regulator window — likely lost) or a "soft" one (internal promise — usually recoverable).
2. For hard ones, ask what's left: never assume lost until you ask, in one polite letter that includes your timeline and asks plainly, "what options still exist at this point?" Some processes allow late filings in specific circumstances — the clerk or the scheme's own guidance usually says so in writing, and asking costs you nothing.
3. For soft ones, adjust and keep going: "I know I missed your window; I'd still like to resolve it." Companies extend things all the time for people who ask plainly and don't pretend the deadline never existed.

## The monthly 10-minute dispute audit

Set one recurring calendar event: first Sunday of the month, 10 minutes, coffee in hand. Go through every open dispute folder and answer four questions:

1. Is there a deadline I haven't mapped yet?
2. Is there a letter I should be sending today/this week?
3. Is there a rung of the ladder I should be advancing?
4. Is any dispute basically dead — and should I formally close it (log a final note, *keep* the folder, mark it closed)?

Keep folders even for closed disputes — the next one in two years is frequently the same company, and old receipts and ref numbers are unexpectedly reusable.

## Blank deadline template (copy into a new dispute)

```markdown
# Deadlines — Dispute: <who/what>
- [ ] Purchase / event date: `<date>`
- [ ] Return window closes: `<date>`  (where: `<source>`)
- [ ] Warranty ends: `<date>`  (where: `<source>`)
- [ ] Written denial / notice received: `<date>`
- [ ] Appeal/dispute window closes: `<date>`  (clock starts from: `<date>`)
- [ ] Letter #1 sent: `<date>` → reply due: `<date>` → follow-up: `<date>`
- [ ] Letter #2 sent: `<date>`
- [ ] Regulator complaint deadline (check locally): `<date>`
- [ ] Small-claims window (check locally): `<date>`
- Reminders set?  T-7: <y/n>   T-2: <y/n>
```
