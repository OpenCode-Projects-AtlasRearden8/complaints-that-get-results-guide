Chapter 6 — AI Tone Review

One sentence to remember: Use AI as your editor, not your author — and redact before you paste.

Why tone review is the right first use

The single highest-leverage thing AI can do for a complaint is also the one thing that doesn't require it to know anything sensitive: reading your letter like a stranger and telling you how it sounds.

You've drafted the letter (Chapter 3). Before you send it, a quick AI pass catches:

  • Tone drift — that paragraph that grew a little hot in draft 2.
  • Missing pieces — no date? no ask? no deadline?
  • Clarity — the sentence that makes sense to you and nobody else.
  • Length — the draft that quietly grew one page too long.

What it's not for: inventing facts, inventing threats ("you'll hear from my lawyer," hmm, unless one is real), "sounding official." Everything substantive comes from you and your records.

The redaction pass (do this first, every time)

Before any letter goes into any AI chat, make a cleaned copy:

  1. Replace your full name → DANIELLE or [NAME].
  2. Replace your address, phone, email → [ADDRESS], [PHONE].
  3. Replace account/policy/claim numbers → [ACCT-####], XXXX-4321.
  4. Replace exact dollar values only if your letter works without them — most letters do; say "the full amount I paid" and keep the real figure in your file, to be re-inserted in the final version.
  5. Trim medical/finances/housing details — they're rarely needed for tone review.
  6. Save as letter-clean-for-AI.txt in 06-reference/. The original stays untouched in 04-letters-sent/.

It takes two minutes and it removes 99% of the oversharing risk. If the letter needs exact numbers to make sense (it usually only needs them for the final send), include them but consider an offline pass instead.

The paste-ready review prompt

Give the AI a job, not just the letter. Copy this into your chat:

You're a writing coach helping me sharpen a customer complaint letter
before I send it. Please do NOT add facts, threats, or "legal" language.
Do NOT expand it. Review the letter below and tell me:

1. TONE: Does it sound firm, calm, and reasonable? Flag any sentence that
   reads angry, sarcastic, or passive-aggressive.
2. CLARITY: Is the ask (what I want) impossible to misunderstand?
3. CLOSING: Point out if anything important is missing:
   my deadline for a reply, a one-sentence summary up top, attachments
   referenced by number, or my contact info.
4. CUT: Which single sentence would you delete, and why?
5. TEST: If a busy clerk reads only the subject line and FIRST sentence,
   will they know what happened and what I want?

Here's the letter (note: I've replaced my personal details and some
numbers; treat them as fine):

<ink your cleaned letter here>

Reply with the numbered points, keep it under 250 words, and end with a
"ready to send?" yes/no.

Now that you have the review in hand, here's how to handle the suggestions.

Reading the AI's review like a grown-up

Apply the suggestions where they improve clarity and calm, ignore them where they water down your ask. Some checks to run yourself:

  • If it suggests "softening the deadline" — usually disagree. Deadlines are why letters work.
  • If it adds "legal-sounding" phrases or threats — cut the addition. Inventing content that could end up in a signed letter is the one thing AI must never do for you.
  • If it suggests expanding the letter, check twice that you didn't put filler there. It's more often you.
  • Note the question "will they know what happened and what I want?" is the one that matters most — that's the letter doing its job.

The hard rules (say these out loud once)

  • Never let AI write new facts. If the AI's suggestion invents a date, a promise, or a policy clause, it must hit the cutting-room floor.
  • Never paste the records themselves (receipts, call logs, medical notes) — only the letter you're sending. Chapter 1's redaction rules apply.
  • Never sign a letter you haven't read and agreed with every sentence of. It's your name on it; AI is the pen you borrow, not the person who signs.
  • If your dispute is a big deal — insurer denial, landlord trouble, claim that could become a court case — reviews are still fine, but real next steps belong to a lawyer or an advice-line, not a chatbot.
