Chapter 1 — Gather Records First

One sentence to remember: In any dispute, the party with the better records usually wins.

Why records beat rage

When a company gets a complaint, someone on the other end asks one question: "Is this person credible?" The fastest way to answer yes is to have dates, names, amounts, and documents ready before you ask for anything. Records:

  • Stop the "he said / she said" spiral.
  • Make your letter easy to act on.
  • Give regulators and small-claims clerks something they can actually use.
  • Calm you down. A folder turns a stressful mess into a checklist.

The 15-minute filing setup

Create one folder per dispute. On your computer or in a cloud drive, build this structure:

dispute-<who>-<year>/
├── 00-log.md            ← your running timeline (most important file)
├── 01-receipts/
├── 02-photos/
├── 03-messages/         ← emails, chat logs, text screenshots
├── 04-letters-sent/     ← every letter you send, as you sent it
├── 05-replies-received/
└── 06-reference/        ← contract, warranty, policy, listing page

Print-and-paper person? Use a single accordion folder with the same tabs. The system matters more than the medium.

The timeline log (your unfair advantage)

00-log.md is just a dated list. Newest on top, oldest at the bottom, facts only. Example:

# Timeline — Dispute with <company>
- 2026-03-14 — Bought model X-200 at Main St location. Receipt in 01-receipts.
- 2026-03-21 — Product stopped working. Photos in 02-photos (IMG_0421–0424).
- 2026-03-22 — Called support ~2:30pm. Spoke to "Amanda" (badge #, recorded later).
              Ref #ABC-4192 given. Told to "wait 3–5 business days." Nothing came.
- 2026-03-28 — Returned to store. Manager "Mr. Lee" declined refund, offered
              coupon. Slip of coupon kept in 01-receipts.
- 2026-03-29 — Sent complaint letter #1 (in 04-letters-sent), email.

Add every call the same day, even a day later. Include: date, time, phone number or location, person's name if any, reference/members, what was said, what was promised, next step promised, and by when. Ask for a reference number on every call — it's free, and it's how you prove you called at all.

What to gather, in order of usefulness

  1. Proof of the deal — receipt, invoice, order confirmation, lease, policy declarations page, listing screenshot.
  2. Proof of the problem — photos with timestamps, error messages, test results, noise/condition notes with dates.
  3. Proof of trying — call logs with dates and numbers, chat exports, reference numbers, prior letters and their replies.
  4. Proof of terms — the warranty text, return policy, lease clause, or policy language you're relying on. Screenshot the policy page as it reads today (pages get edited).

Rules that make your records strong

  • Facts, not feelings, in the log. "Rude" is a feeling; "hung up while I was saying 'I'd like a supervisor'" is a fact.
  • Photograph everything with context. Wide shot, medium shot, close-up. Include a plain note or paper with the date visible when it matters.
  • Screenshot web pages and prices. Chat transcripts expire; screenshots don't.
  • Don't doctor anything. Never crop, "clean up," or partially enhance evidence. It will come back to bite you. If evidence contains personal info, redact the copies you share — never the originals you keep.
  • Back it up. Two places minimum (e.g., local + cloud). A dispute is the worst possible time to have a fried laptop.

Redaction: keep private things private

Before you share records with third parties (a regulator, a company's legal inbox, AI tools — see Chapter 6), make a cleaned copy:

  • Hide full account numbers → XXXX-1234.
  • Hide full addresses, birthdates, SSN/national IDs, medical details, and exact dollar amounts where not needed.
  • Cross out or blur — and save under a new name like report-clean.pdf. Never redact the original.

The pre-flight checklist

Before you move on to Chapter 2, you should be able to say yes to all of these:

  • [ ] One folder exists for this dispute.
  • [ ] Timeline log has at least 3 dated entries.
  • [ ] I have proof of the deal and proof of the problem.
  • [ ] Every call made so far is logged with a reference number (or a note of why there isn't one).
  • [ ] Copies are backed up; originals are safe.
  • [ ] I have a redacted set ready for anything I might share externally.

If all checked, you're better prepared than most people who win these fights.
